Banco Chinchorro snorkeling and diving

Banco Chinchorro snorkeling and diving trips leave from Mahahual for a protected reef atoll about 31 km offshore. It is a full day on a boat, and it depends on the weather, so plan it for early in your stay.

What Banco Chinchorro is

Banco Chinchorro is a reef atoll in the Caribbean, about 31 km east of Mahahual across a deep channel. Mexico declared it a biosphere reserve on 19 July 1996. It joined UNESCO's World Network of Biosphere Reserves in 2003 and was listed as a Ramsar wetland of international importance in 2004. UNESCO describes it as the largest platform-type reef complex in Mexico.

The reserve covers about 144,000 hectares, and less than 1% of that is dry land. The land is three groups of small islands, or cays: Cayo Norte, Cayo Centro and Cayo Lobos. Cayo Centro is the largest, and American crocodiles live there.

Shipwrecks and the 40 Cannons

The reef has caught ships for centuries, and Mexico's National Institute of Anthropology and History (INAH) treats the atoll as an underwater museum. The best-known wreck is the 40 Cannons (40 Cañones), a 17th-century Spanish galleon on the northwest side of the atoll. It lies in about 8 m of water, with bronze cannons scattered across the sand, shallow enough to see while snorkelling.

Do not confuse it with the 40 Cañones Reef, a separate dive site near Mahahual.

Snorkelling at Chinchorro

Snorkelling is allowed only in designated zones, and your guide will keep the group to them. The sites are shallow and the water is usually clear. The long boat ride is the hardest part of the day for most people.

Diving at Chinchorro

Divers stay on the sheltered western side of the atoll. The eastern side faces the open sea, and rough surf there can make diving difficult or impossible. Permits to take divers into the reserve are hard to come by, so not every dive shop in Mahahual goes. Ask early, and bring your certification card.

How a day trip works

Trips leave Mahahual in the morning, usually around 8:00, and get back in the middle of the afternoon. The boat ride takes about 1.5 to 2 hours each way, depending on the sea. Go with an operator licensed to work in the reserve.

The reserve charges a fee set by CONANP, Mexico's protected areas agency. Some operators include it in the price and others charge it separately, so ask when you book.

Operators cancel when the sea is rough, and many need a minimum number of people to go. Book for early in your stay so there is room to move the trip to another day.

Best time to go

The dry season, from November to April, usually brings steadier weather, calmer seas and clearer water, and the water averages 25 to 28 °C in winter. The rainy season runs from June to October, when the crossing can be choppier, and August to October is hurricane season. Our winter season, November to April, falls in the calmer months.

What to bring

Reef-safe sunscreen, a hat, a long-sleeved top for the boat, and seasickness tablets if you need them. We provide beach towels.

After a long day on the water, it helps to come back to a full kitchen and A/C in every bedroom. Our 3 bedroom apartments sleep up to 9, so the whole group can stay together.

Questions

How far is Banco Chinchorro from Mahahual?

About 31 km east of Mahahual. The boat ride takes about 1.5 to 2 hours each way, depending on the sea.

Can beginners snorkel at Banco Chinchorro?

Snorkelling is in shallow, designated zones with a guide, and the 40 Cannons wreck lies in about 8 m of water. The long and sometimes choppy boat ride is the harder part, so take seasickness tablets if you need them.

Is the reserve fee included in the tour price?

It depends on the operator. Some include the CONANP reserve fee and others charge it separately, so ask when you book.

When is the best time to visit Banco Chinchorro?

The dry season, November to April, usually has calmer seas and clearer water. August to October is hurricane season.

What happens if the sea is too rough?

Operators cancel trips in rough seas. Book early in your stay so you can move the trip to another day.
